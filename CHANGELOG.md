# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **`gcd`, and `to_s` learns a base** ([#23](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/23)) — Euclid on magnitudes (the answer never carries a sign, a zero yields the other side's magnitude), and `to_s(2)` through `to_s(36)` write lowercase digits with the sign out front; out of bounds refuses with Ruby's own words, `invalid radix 1`. All shapes probed on 4.0.6, including the negatives the import's examples left unasked. Three more holes close: 33 remain across 66 pending runs.

- **The float predicates and the rounding family** ([#23](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/23)) — seven holes close: `zero?` widens to Float, `nan?` and `finite?` arrive as themselves, and `floor`/`ceil`/`round`/`truncate` each answer an Integer with Ruby 4.0.6's shapes (half away from zero, toward zero — probed, and Rust's `f64` agrees arm for arm). One deliberate divergence, ledgered in nil-and-optionals.md: `infinite?` is a predicate proper answering `true`/`false`, not Ruby's `nil`/`1`/`-1` — a direction wearing a predicate's name is unusable where nil is not falsy, and the divergence is detected, not silent. The compiler's arms ride the host builtins; the inventory drops to 36 holes across 72 pending runs.

- **`next` after a dot is a method name — `succ`'s third spelling arrives** ([#79](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/79)) — loop control is a statement and can never stand where a receiver's message goes, so the two readings of `next` never meet and no guess is involved (principle 3's grammar-position disambiguation, the same reason Ruby allows `1.class`). Both parsers admit exactly one keyword in dot position — `next`, narrowly, not keywords wholesale — and `page.next` answers what `page.succ` answers, the compiler riding the host's `succ` so the synonyms cannot drift. The succession spec pins all three spellings against each other; `value_methods.pdx` pins both oracles differentially; language.md's loop-control paragraph carries the dot-position exception.

- **`succ` and `pred` arrive, and they are the first fruit of the corrected principle** ([#23](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/23), [#79](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/79)) — the neighbors are synonyms for `+ 1` and `- 1`, built *because* one behavior may have many spellings (principle 3): sometimes the natural sentence says `total + 1`, sometimes `page.succ`. One arm apiece in the seed, and the compiler's arms ride the host builtins so the behaviors cannot drift. The succession spec un-pends into four examples, `value_methods.pdx` pins both oracles differentially, and the inventory drops to 43 holes across 86 pending runs. `next` as the third spelling waits at #79 — it is a keyword, and taking it as a method name needs its own look.

- **Many names, one behavior — principle 3 gains its converse** — "one name per job" had crept into [#79](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/79)'s framing and a CHANGELOG entry as "the house lean," and it never was one: expressiveness through synonyms — `total + 1` in one sentence, `page.succ` in another — is a good part of Ruby that Portland keeps on purpose, for joy and for migration both. Principle 3 now states the real rule with its direction explicit: **one spelling never has two behaviors; one behavior may have many spellings.** What Ruby got wrong is a word behaving one of many ways depending on runtime context, and that is the half Portland refuses. #79 still decides which alias families ship, family by family; whether synonyms are allowed at all is no longer a question.

- **"The trio" retires as a name; the thing is called the compiler** — the collection in `compiler/` outgrew its headcount the day the checker made it four, and a name that counts pieces breaks every time a piece is added. The docs, comments, tests, and scripts now say what the thing is (the compiler) rather than how many files it takes; the Rust program stays the seed, "the seed compiler" where the contrast needs spelling out. Sentences that used "the real compiler" or "the actual compiler" to mean *the part that doesn't exist yet* now name that part precisely: diagnostics and inference belong to the checker ([#9](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/9)), emitting binaries to the back end ([#5](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/5)). Frozen texts — ADRs, `docs/history/`, past CHANGELOG entries — keep "trio" as the word of their day, and ROADMAP's "transitional-tooling trio" keeps counting the three issues it actually counts.

- **The checker arrives — the trio overtakes the seed for the first time** ([#9](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/9) increment 1, [ADR 0034](docs/adr/0034-2026-08-11-the-checker-and-the-oracle-succession.md)) — `compiler/checker.pdx`, a fourth walker run before evaluation, carrying the narrowest check that exists: ADR 0022's closed vocabularies, statically. A dead-branch `:paid(whenever:)` now refuses at build while the seed runs the same program to completion — architecture.md's third row, live, exactly as predicted. Construction typos keep the seed's *runtime* wordings byte for byte (only the timing moves); the pattern typo — `in :payed`, the branch that silently never fires — gets **the trio's first original diagnostic** (`in :payed can never match — no enum declares a case :payed`), because the seed answers that bug with silence. ADR 0034 settles the doctrine principle 7 needed: never hand-write what an oracle can produce; where no oracle exists, the deciding ADR is the oracle and the test pins its exact words — and principles.md carries the amendment. The milestone test asserts both halves of four programs: the seed runs them, the trio refuses them. `compiler/check.pdx` is the checker's own door; hosted runs pay one extra AST walk (~5s across the spec suite); the checker declines wherever it cannot tell, which is principle 4 promoted from apology to method.

- **The import's decisions get their issues** — six questions the first pass surfaced or made concrete, each now a commitment rather than a silence: regex ([#74](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/74)), string semantics with the Unicode pile as its pull ([#75](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/75)), ordering and Comparable-as-a-trait ([#76](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/76)), whether a block can be held in a value — asked on purpose after `core/proc` went unimportable, with the honest note that the restriction is load-bearing for write-site `return` ([#77](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/77)), the stdlib story ([#78](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/78)), and the alias-cluster policy that recurs at every type ([#79](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/79)). ROADMAP's surface list carries all six.

- **The ruby/spec import completes its first full pass** ([#23](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/23)) — the `language/` and iteration sweeps close it out. `case` gains the comma-separated `when` it always had but never pinned; the block-taking library beyond the trio's twelve arms turns out to be all inventory (`any?`/`all?`/`none?`, `find`, `sort_by`, `tally`, `zip`, `each_slice`, `flat_map` — each noted as a heavier build, a new arm in the trio's deliberately-repetitive dispatch, not just a seed builtin). Five undecided spellings the pass surfaced are filed as [#73](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/73) rather than pended — `until`, `||=`, the postfix loop modifiers, `loop do`, subjectless `case` — since the inventory holds what is owed and nothing is owed until ruled. The ternary and multiple assignment needed no filing: one stays refused by the standing never-guess caution, the other's Portland spelling (`[1, 2] => [a, b]`) was specced weeks ago. **The pass in sum: 624 example runs, 0 failures, 90 pending — 45 distinct holes, every one carrying its semantics in its string — plus four issues (#71, #72, #73, and ADR 0033's corner) and the Unicode pile the string-semantics ADR has been waiting for.**

- **Range and Symbol, imported** ([#23](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/23)) — Range adds `step` and `min`/`max` (exclusive end honored) to the inventory, with `size`/`count` riding the alias-policy flag rather than pending twice. Symbol is the opposite of a hole list: `to_s` gets its example — the one sanctioned crossing into text, colon shed, odd keys included — and the rest of upstream's directory (`length`, `upcase`, `succ`, `<=>`, `casecmp`) is recorded as *exclusion by doctrine*: a symbol is a name, not a string that happens to be short (ADR 0023), and the refusals are the feature.

- **String, imported — the ASCII half, and the Unicode pile the ADR has been waiting for** ([#23](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/23)) — the deeper library is entirely unbuilt, so the trimmers (`strip`, `lstrip`/`rstrip`, `chomp`), the searches (`index`-of-a-substring, `count`, `lines`), and the other case moves (`capitalize`, `swapcase`) join the inventory; `sub`/`gsub` stay behind with regex, since even their plain-string arities lead straight to the pattern question. And `spec/string/unicode_spec.pdx` is the parked pile the plan promised: what both oracles answer today is recorded as fact — `"café".length` is 4 and `"🇺🇸".length` is 2, scalar counting by accident of the Rust floor, byte-identical hosted and direct — while the four questions whose expected values are *unknowable until decided* (what is a character; is comparison normalized; casing beyond ASCII) sit as blockless pendings naming the string-semantics ADR as what they wait for. Principle 6's pull, with receipts attached.

- **Hash, imported — small surface, sharp triage** ([#23](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/23)) — the library beyond `[]`/`length`/`empty?`/`key?`/`keys`/`values`/`each` turns out to be nothing at all, so the batch is four pendings and two dispositions. Owed: `merge` (the functional update writ large, right side winning collisions, both originals untouched) and the deeper lookups `dig` (a maybe at every step) and `to_a` (pairs in insertion order). Not holes: `fetch` stays dropped forever — ADR 0010 kept its stored-nil rule without it and the or-guard is the miss-takes-a-default spelling — and the alias cluster (`size`, `has_key?`, `include?`, `member?`) is flagged as a *policy* question rather than pended: whether Portland ships Ruby's alias families at all ([#79](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/79) decides; this entry originally miscalled "one name per job" the house lean — it is not, per principle 3). The inventory stands at 25 holes across 50 pending runs.

- **Array, imported — and the read-only library turns out thinner than it looked** ([#23](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/23)) — `sort` grows duplicate and trivial-case coverage, and the import's sharpest find is that it sorts *integers only*: strings refuse (`sort needs an array of integers`), so lexical sort joins the inventory. So do eight more: `reverse` (the string twin exists, the array one does not), `uniq`, `flatten`, `compact`, `count`, `index`-of-a-value, and counted `first`/`last`. The bang twins (`reverse!`, `uniq!`) stay behind forever — values never mutate (ADR 0015), the ledger owns that story, and the spec headers say so once each. Mixed-type sort refuses on both oracles where Ruby raises its comparison error — the same behavior class, recorded. The inventory stands at 21 distinct holes across 42 pending runs.

- **Float, imported** ([#23](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/23)) — arithmetic with IEEE's imprecision pinned honestly (`0.1 + 0.2` prints `0.30000000000000004` on both oracles), the edges Ruby answers with words (`1.0 / 0.0` is `Infinity`, signed; `0.0 / 0.0` is `NaN`; NaN equals nothing, itself included — all byte-identical across the oracles already), and ordering across the integer line. The inventory grows by seven: the rounding family (`floor`, `ceil`, `round` half-away-from-zero, `truncate` toward it — values run against Ruby 4.0.6, not recalled) and the predicates (`zero?` is integer-only today; `nan?`, `finite?`, `infinite?` have no spellings). The import's second literal find is filed as [#72](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/72): `0xff` lexes as `0` then `xff` — prefixed integer literals don't exist, with the honest note that ADR 0003's bitwise decision may bear on how much pull hex really has here.

- **Exponent lands: `**` and `pow`, a minus before it applying last** ([ADR 0033](docs/adr/0033-2026-08-11-exponent-is-starstar-and-pow.md); both oracles, differentially pinned, specced) — found missing by the Integer import, surveyed rather than recalled, and re-decided in review: a first draft refused every negated base on ES2016's precedent, and review rejected it — a declared precedence is one grammar reading, not two, and tie-goes-to-Ruby outranks the caution anyway. So `-2 ** 2` is `-4` and `-x ** 2` is `-(x ** 2)`, Ruby's answers and mathematics'. **One corner still refuses, because there Ruby contradicts itself**: probed live, `-5.abs` is `5` and `-5 ** 2` is `-25` but `-5.abs ** 2` is `25` — add `.abs` and the sign flips. Portland shows the menu instead of importing the unrecitable rule or silently diverging from it. Everything else: right-associative towers (`2 ** 3 ** 2` is 512), binding above `*`, `pow` as the named twin, floats through `powf`, the rational and overflow refusals with rewrites named, magnitude-one bases answering Ruby at any height. Retired on the way: the trio never fused negative float literals, so `-2.5.abs` answered `-2.5` hosted and `2.5` direct — silent until the `**` work made it loud; both oracles fuse now. A corpus pass to size the refused corner is queued on [#23](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/23)'s arc.

- **Backend architecture sketch banked for #5** ([docs/history/2026-07-24-pliron-mlir-hybrid.md](docs/history/2026-07-24-pliron-mlir-hybrid.md)) — a pliron/MLIR hybrid: pliron (MLIR's dialect/op/pass architecture, natively in safe Rust) hosts the Portland-specific middle IR; real MLIR runs below a textual seam as stock pinned binaries, owning the hardware arrows (`linalg`/`vector`, ARM's own `arm_sme`, `gpu` kernel outlining). The shape is rustc's HIR/MIR→LLVM with a smarter boundary. Stages 1–2 could run pliron-only (CPU, serial `together` per #11), MLIR joining exactly when #13 does — the thesis is never bet on the young dependency. Caveats recorded: no public AIR backend exists in LLVM/MLIR under *any* framework; MLIR's textual format churns, so the seam needs pinned versions and golden tests; the fatter Rust floor cuts against the Rubinius creed unless the pliron layer is Stage 1–2 scaffolding. Distribution: Brewfile for compiler devs only, vendored toolchain binaries for Portland programmers, nothing at runtime. Exploration, not a decision — #5 owns every call in it.

- **Integer, second batch — abs, conversions, unary minus, and the inventory grows** ([#23](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/23)) — `abs` across sign and zero, `to_f` keeping its sign, `to_s` writing digits back out, unary minus on bound names both directions. Three new pendings join the owed list: `to_s` with a base (parses, no builtin arity), `succ`, and `pred`. Two exclusions named where they happen rather than pended: unary plus does not parse and buys nothing, and `**` is a *decision* rather than a hole — ADR 0003 sent the bitwise operators to named methods, so whether exponent is `**` or `pow` is surface for an ADR, not an inventory line.

- **The ruby/spec import begins: Integer, first batch** ([#23](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/23)) — grown from `core/integer`'s even/odd/zero, divide, modulo, lt/lteq/gt/gteq, and plus/minus/multiply specs: parity below zero, floored division's four sign corners and the small-negative floor (`-1 / 10` is `-1`), the modulo edges truncation would get wrong (`-200 % 256` is `56`), comparisons across the float line (`5 < 4.999`), and multiplication past 32 bits. Each file cites its upstream source; each exclusion is named where it happens — bignum literals refuse at parse (past i64), `<=>` and the `coerce` protocol are missing syntax and missing model rather than pending holes. `gcd_spec.pdx` holds the inventory's first two `pending` entries, and the import's first find is filed: underscored numeric literals (`5_280`) do not lex.

- **The spec harness grows `pending` — a hole we owe, held in place** (for [#23](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/23)'s import) — the example stays written where it belongs, prints one countable `* PENDING` line, and its block never runs. Not-running is the only semantics available, not a taste choice: rspec's true pending runs the block and alerts when it starts passing, which needs a failure to be caught, and here `expect` prints rather than raises while a missing builtin panics the whole file — so this is mspec's skip wearing the honest name. The body still parses on both oracles (blocks parse whether or not they run), so `pending` holds missing builtins and wrong behavior, never missing syntax. `script/spec` tallies pendings per file and in the grand total, silent when there are none and never failing on them — `script/spec | grep PENDING` is the standing compatibility-hole inventory the ruby/spec import will now generate.

- **The spec suite drops its wrapper parens: `expect(config).to have_key(:name)`** — ADR 0032's payoff, 309 assertion lines across all 116 spec files, rewritten by a one-off Ruby script with a balanced-paren scan so nothing chained or mid-expression was touched. The transcripts are unchanged — same examples, same tallies — which is the proof the rewrite moved spelling and nothing else. The matcher keeps its parens on purpose: `.to eq(y)` is rspec's own dominant style, and the fully-bare form is the line ADR 0032 left uncrossed.

- **Dotted calls take paren-less arguments — `expect(x).to eq(y)` parses** ([ADR 0032](docs/adr/0032-2026-08-11-dotted-calls-take-paren-less-arguments.md), closing [#68](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/68); both oracles, differentially pinned, specced) — the mirror of the bare command call one dot deeper: the same start test, the same argument loop with keywords and commas, the same refusals where two readings exist, each wording shared byte-for-byte. Adjacency nests to the innermost call and a `do` past the arguments belongs to the outermost, Ruby's parses verified against 4.0.6 before adoption; `a.b -1` refuses where Ruby warns "ambiguous first argument" — the warning wearing a refusal, the established move. The seed guards the block attach with its `command_arguments` flag, the trio truncates the argument token list at the opener — different mechanisms, same rule. One line stays uncrossed on purpose and the ADR records it: a *bare* command as an argument (`.to eq y`) does not parse until something real pulls for it; the matcher keeps its parens, which is rspec's own dominant style. An `evaluator_dotted_commands` differential, three wording cases, and `spec/method/paren_less_spec.pdx` — written in the new syntax it pins — carry the coverage.

- **The spec suite shares its harness instead of re-parsing it 116 times — `script/test` goes 38.6s → 15.2s** ([#69](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/69)) — and the issue's own premise had to be disproved first. It claimed a hosted spec pays 0.42s to rebuild the trio; isolating that number says loading the trio is **0.02s** and the other 0.40s is `spec_helper.pdx`, which every spec re-requires into its own fresh scope. A driver that only loaded the trio once was built, measured at **52.8s against 37s**, and thrown away — batching evaluations into one process is *slower*, presumably allocation pressure that process teardown was reclaiming for free. What works is sharing the harness: `spec/run_specs.pdx` evaluates `spec_helper.pdx` once and hands each spec a scope built from it, seeding `__loaded__` so the spec's own `require_relative` answers false. Safe precisely where sharing a *spec's* scope would not be — six `struct Token`s and three `def apply`s collide across files with different bodies, so each spec still gets a scope of its own. Output is byte-identical to one-process-per-spec, verified by diff. The language-spec test drops 37.7s → 10.4s and `script/spec` runs in 13.1s; both split the batch back on the driver's `===` markers to keep per-file tallies, and a spec the batch never reached reports a failure rather than tallying empty and reading as green. Pinned by deliberately breaking an example and watching the test catch it.

- **A required path canonicalises, and a recorded divergence retires** — the prerequisite the runner needed, and a debt paid on the way. The trio resolved `require_relative` by joining strings, so `"library"` and `"./library"` were different load-once keys and the same file loaded twice where the seed, which asks the filesystem to canonicalise, loaded it once. `canonical_path` now walks the segments and cancels each `..` against the name before it, so every spelling of one file shares a key — which is also what lets the driver seed one `__loaded__` entry for specs nested at any depth. The comment in `evaluator.pdx` that recorded the gap (principle 4 — a gap is written down, never silent) is down to one divergence from two, and `requires_library.pdx` grows the long-way-round spellings so the fixture suite pins the agreement on both oracles.

- **The rule specs split too, one construct to a file** — the twelve remaining top-level files become `control_flow/`, `pattern_matching/`, `optionals/`, `failure/`, `struct/`, `mutable/`, `method/`, `namespace/`, `enum/`, `trait/`, `together/`, and `word_operators/`. Where the value types split by method, these split by the thing they pin: `control_flow/unwinding_spec.pdx`, `pattern_matching/pin_spec.pdx`, `optionals/safe_navigation_spec.pdx`, `struct/definable_new_spec.pdx`. `inspect_spec.pdx` stays at the top level and stays whole, because it already is what the reorganization is for — one file pinning one function. The suite is **116 files, 532 example runs**.

  Splitting kept finding gaps, as it did for the value types: `unless` answering nil when its condition holds, an array pattern matching `[]` by shape, alternation and pins *missing*, `nil?`/`some?` asked of nil itself — the one dispatch absence allows, and previously asked only of 42 — an enum case differing from its siblings, a constant named through its module, and a module function written without the `self.` marker beside one written with it (ADR 0031's non-difference, which had only ever been pinned in one direction).

  The cost is the one already measured, now paid 116 times: `script/test` goes **38.6s → 59.8s**, almost entirely the 0.42s a hosted spec file spends loading the trio before it runs a line. That is now the dominant term in the suite, and the fix is unchanged — a runner that loads the trio once for many specs rather than once per spec.

- **A heredoc is a String, so its spec lives under `spec/string/`** — the reorganization moved `%w[]` to `array/word_array_spec.pdx` on the grounds that a literal belongs with the type it builds, then left the heredoc at the top level: the same test, answered two ways in one commit. `spec/string/heredoc_spec.pdx` now reads `context "String" → describe "the heredoc"`, which is what the path was already claiming. The rest of the top level survives the test on purpose — `control_flow`, `pattern_matching`, `mutable`, `namespace` and their kin pin rules rather than the methods of a value.

- **The value-type specs go one method to a file** — `spec/string_spec.pdx` becomes `spec/string/length_spec.pdx`, `case_spec.pdx`, `split_spec.pdx`, and nine more; the same for `array/`, `hash/`, `range/`, `symbol/`, and the rest of `number/`. This extends the shape `spec/number/integer/division_spec.pdx` already had rather than inventing one — a file named for the method it pins, `context "String" → describe "#length"` inside, so the path and the transcript say the same thing. 6 files became 44, the suite is 65 files and **516 example runs**, and the reorganization added examples the old grouping had swallowed: `#empty?` on a single space, `#split` with an absent separator, `#sort` leaving its receiver alone, `key?` on a key whose value is nil, `0.even?`.

  The cost is measured, and it is the file count rather than the examples: a hosted spec file pays **0.42s** to load the trio before it runs a line, plus about 0.024s an assertion. So the language-spec test goes **19.4s → 37.7s** and `script/test` **20.5s → 38.6s**, almost all of it that fixed cost paid 65 times instead of 27. The per-file 20s tripwire is untouched — the slowest file is under a second — but the suite is now sensitive to file count in a way worth knowing before the split goes further, and the fix, whenever it is wanted, is a runner that loads the trio once for many specs rather than once per spec.

- **The spec suite reads like a sentence: `expect(x).to(eq(y))`** — the harness swaps its two-argument `expect` for rspec's chain, and all 27 spec files move with it. `.to` is doing a preposition's job, which is the whole difference: `expect(x, y)` — and every xUnit `assert_equal x, y` — is verb-object-object with nothing joining the two, and no amount of renaming fixes the word order. The same instinct already puts `.of` and `.from` on constructor entry points (ADR 0031). Nine matchers cover the suite: `eq`, `be_true`/`be_false`, `be_nil`/`be_some`/`be_failure`/`be_empty`, `have_key`/`have_length`, and `contain` — `include` being a keyword ADR 0028 spent on traits, so rspec's spelling of that one is unspellable here.

  The migration paid for itself twice over. **Roughly forty assertions were packing two independent facts into one comparison** — `expect([config.key?(:name), config.key?(:nope)], [true, false])` — because equality was the only matcher; unpacked, a failure names which half broke instead of printing two arrays to diff by eye, and the count went 231 → 271. **Each matcher owns its own wording**, so failures read `expected a key :port in {name: "pdx"}` and `expected length 5, got 2` rather than everything degrading to `expected X, got Y`. And splitting `struct_spec`'s packed example **found missing ADR 0031 coverage**: nothing asserted that `Badge.new("")` *is* a failure, only that the fallback worked.

  Two costs, recorded rather than buried. The hosted spec run roughly doubled — the integration suite goes **10.75s → 20.48s** — because every assertion became a struct allocation plus two dispatches where it had been one `==`; the per-file 20s tripwire is untouched at ~0.7s a file, and direct runs are unaffected. And the parens around each matcher are ceremony that [#68](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/68) would remove: `expect(x).to eq(y)` needs paren-less arguments on dotted calls, which the language does not have yet.

  The predicate matchers are one struct apiece differing by a single method call, which is what rspec's `method_missing` buys written out — tolerable at four, not at forty, and the clearest case yet for what compile-time macros ([#14](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/14)) would actually be for.

- **The `class` ledger closes: `docs/ruby/classes.md`** — the single biggest thing a migrating Rubyist greps for, written now that job 3 built so it could be complete rather than promissory: all four jobs of Ruby's `class` mapped to their homes (struct, trait, `def self.` with definable `new`, and the honest job-4-pending-[#11](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/11) note), with the migration moves spelled out — `initialize` to `def self.new`, `super` to `fields`, `class << self` flattened. language.md's struct section gains the construction paragraph and its decided-not-built line retires; ROADMAP's table gains the 0031 row.

- **ADR 0031 is built — type functions, definable `new`, and `fields`, both oracles** ([#67](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/67), the build arc; differentially pinned, specced) — `def self.name` in a struct body registers `Token::of` under the struct's namespace, so the existing namespace rung dispatches it with no new machinery and bare names inside resolve outward from the struct, sibling type functions included. A defined `new` replaces the raw constructor everywhere — positional signatures included, which raw `new` never took — and `fields(...)`, legal only inside it, is the raw kwargs-in-fields-out layer, cleared across every other call boundary so nothing it calls inherits it. A module-body `def self.` is a plain def, as decided. Eight refusal wordings shared byte-for-byte across the oracles: `fields` outside a defined `new` (and reached through a helper), a program's own `fields` colliding inside one, positional `fields`, top-level and trait `def self.`, the instances-and-type name collision, and the reserved set. An `evaluator_type_functions` differential, five new spec examples in `struct_spec`, and the error-wording differential grown by eight pin it. The seed's own ADR 0001 enforcement caught the build's one bug — a rebound immutable local in the evaluator — which is the language protecting its own compiler.

- **ADR 0031 gains the module edge from review** — `def self.name` in a *module* body is accepted and identical to plain `def`: Ruby spells `Statistics.mean` that way, Portland's module `def` already means it (ADR 0021), so accepting the redundant marker is a non-difference that lets migrating modules compile unchanged. - **The brace peek answers three ways, not two**, because "could be a hash" was hiding a distinction that matters. `twice {name: 1}` was reported as two readings — and it has one: a label cannot start a statement, so there is no block body to read `{name: 1}` as. The menu invented an ambiguity and then offered `twice() {name: 1}` as the rewrite, **which does not parse**. That is the third time in two days that a never-guess menu named a dead end, and the second in code I had just written; it was caught by being asked "what are the multiple readings of this example?" rather than by a test.

- The three answers are *block*, *hash*, *either*, and each row was settled by asking the seed whether the body is a statement rather than by reasoning: `name: 1` is not, `{name: 1}` is, `"a" => 1` is (a one-line match assertion, ADR 0013), `"a" => 1, "b" => 2` is not. So `{name: 1}` and `{"a" => 1, "b" => 2}` are hashes only; `{}` and `{"a" => 1}` are genuinely either; everything else is a block. Every rewrite the three messages name was then run, which is the check that was missing all along.

- **One reading does not always mean accept**, which amends ADR 0024's rule as written. A single *block* reading attaches; a single *hash* reading is still refused, and told rather than asked — `` is a hash, not a block — a block body cannot start with a label. Write foo({ ... }) ``. Accepting it would make `foo { ... }` a block for most bodies and a hash argument for pair-shaped ones, which is the unrecitable rule the ADR exists to avoid, and nothing is lost because `foo name: 1` already passes keyword arguments.

- `twice {{name: 1}}` is a block holding a hash, on both oracles — the first element inside the outer braces is a `{`, which cannot start a pair. **Ruby 4.0.6 reads it identically**, which was checked, along with the three refusals: Ruby accepts `foo {}` and `foo { "a" => 1 }` as blocks and rejects `foo {name: 1}` outright. So the ledger records **two** divergences where it had recorded one, and notes that Ruby refuses the third for a different reason than we do.

- [#39](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/39) filed for a divergence that surfaced in passing and is wider than its documentation suggests: the trio prints a guest symbol as `[__symbol__, name]`, so **any hash with symbol keys** renders differently between the oracles — `{name => 1}` against `{[__symbol__, name] => 1}`. `architecture.md` records it as "a program that displays one raw", which reads like an edge case; ADR 0023 made shorthand keys the ordinary way to write a hash, so it is not one. The differential harness compares stdout, so the next example that happens to print a hash fails and looks like a bug in whatever it was testing.

- **Brace blocks attach wherever `do` blocks do** ([ADR 0024](docs/adr/0024-2026-07-26-brace-after-a-call-name.md), built in the seed and the trio, differentially pinned). The line that drove it now runs: `repeat { greet name: "pdx" }`. So does `repeat() { ... }`, and `render(config) { ... }`, and `render(config() { ... })` — none of which parsed before, in any spelling. Until today a brace block could only be given to a *dot* call.

- **Two of ADR 0016's three suggested rewrites did not parse**, which is the part that turned a nicety into a repair. Its worked example has been telling authors to write `render(config { ... })` and `render(config) { ... }` since the day it landed, and both were syntax errors — so the never-guess menu, whose whole promise is "one round, never a dialogue", sent you to a second unrelated error. Measured rather than reasoned, and the fix is what makes the menu true.

- A third flaw in the same menu, subtler: `render(config { ... })` would still have been **ambiguous if it had parsed** — is the brace config's hash argument or its block? Both menus now say `render(config() { ... })`, which parses and has one reading. ADR 0016's text and the ledger are corrected in place, since a rewrite that does not work is not a rewrite.

- What stays refused is what genuinely has two readings: `{}` (empty hash or empty block), `{name: 1}`, `{ "a" => 1 }`. `foo {}` is the one residual Ruby divergence — valid there, refused here with both rewrites named — and it is in the ledger.

- The oracles reach it differently, again. The seed extends its `command_arguments` flag from `do` to `{`; the trio, holding no parser state, finds the opener up front and parses the arguments against a token list that stops there. That needed two exclusions the seed gets for free: a `{` after a **comma** is a hash literal argument, not an opener (`render one, {name: 1}` still parses), and a `{` after a **dot call** was never ambiguous (`puts counting.reject { it.even? }` — which is in `value_methods.pdx`, and is how the too-broad first attempt got caught).

- **The brace peek reads the first pair position, not the whole body.** `twice { greet name: "pdx" }` was called ambiguous because the peek saw `name:` *somewhere* inside and concluded the braces might be a hash. They cannot be: a hash's first element has to be `label: value` or `value => value`, and this one starts with `greet` followed by an identifier. `config = { greet name: "pdx" }` is `expected => in hash literal`, so the parser already knew — the peek just was not asking in the right place. Now it asks at the front, and stops at the first comma or newline, since a hash literal cannot span lines either.

- This was the peek trimming the menu **wrongly**, which is the failure principle 3 names: peek to shorten the question, never to answer it. Yesterday's fix taught it about shorthand keys and made it too eager in the process; both halves are now pinned, the shorthand one and this one, on both oracles.

- **A bare `{` right after a call's name says what it could mean.** It used to report `expected a newline after statement, got Some(Token { leading_space: true, kind: LeftBrace, text: "{" })` — a token dump in a never-guess position, which is the one place the language promises a menu with rewrites. `starts_command` simply had no `LeftBrace` arm, so the call never became a command call and the statement-boundary check picked up the pieces. It now names both readings, a block for the call or a hash argument to it, exactly as the position one token later has always done.

- **And it trims that menu honestly, which turned out to matter.** With no argument in between there is no inner call to own the block, so `expecting { |item| item }` has *one* reading, not two — a `|` rules the hash out. The first draft said "could be two things" for it, which is the peek picking a winner by omission in reverse: claiming an ambiguity that is not there. Where the menu is one long the error stops asking and starts telling: `` `{` after a paren-less call is this call's block — write expecting() { ... } or expecting do ... end ``.

- **A pre-existing bug found on the way, and it was the more serious one.** `braces_could_be_a_hash` looked only for `=>`, so it predated ADR 0023's shorthand — `render config {name: 1}` was reported as "a block — but whose?", **dropping the hash reading entirely** when `{name: 1}` is as much a hash as `{"name" => 1}`. That is the peek answering the question instead of shortening it, which principle 3 names specifically. Both oracles had it; both are fixed. A keyword argument inside a block body now widens the menu by one line, which is the safe direction of wrong.

- The trio did not merely word this differently, it **crashed**: `IdentifierNode has no field value`, an internal error where the seed had a diagnostic. Both wordings now agree on all four shapes, pinned in `portland_evaluator_reports_the_seed_wording_on_errors` rather than left to a manual diff.

- Left undecided on purpose, and it is a real question: with one reading, a `{` after a paren-less name could just as well be **accepted** as that call's block instead of refused with instructions — Ruby reads it that way, and ADR 0016 says braces mean what they look like wherever readings do not collide. That is a decision, not a diagnostic, so it wants an ADR rather than a quiet parser change.

- **`describe "subject" do` parses** — a `do` block on a paren-less call, seed and trio, differentially pinned. The spec suite dropped its parens throughout, which is the whole reason to want it: `describe "Array#first" do` reads like rubyspec, and `describe("Array#first") do` never did.

- No new decision was needed, only the deferred half of an old one. ADR 0016 already analysed this position and said `do/end` is unchanged: a bare `{` has three genuine readings and gets a menu, while `do` has exactly one owner in Ruby — the **outermost** call. The seed's `blocks on paren-less calls aren't supported yet` was a "not built", not a "not allowed", and it is gone.

- **The subtlety, and it cost the second test:** `outer inner do ... end` must hand the block to `outer`, and the naive change hands it to `inner`, because the argument's own expression parser reaches the `do` first and claims it. That is precisely Ruby's `{` rule showing up where its `do` rule belongs — the exact confusion ADR 0016 exists to prevent, reproduced by accident inside the parser. Both spellings are now pinned: `hosting "a string argument" do` and `hosting inner_value do`, where only the second can catch this.

- The two oracles solve it differently, and the difference is structural rather than stylistic. The seed carries a `command_arguments` flag saying "an argument may not claim a block", cleared inside parens and inside a block body so each opens a context of its own — which is why `outer(inner do ... end)` still gives the block to `inner`. The trio's parser is functional and holds no such state, so it **hides the `do` instead**: it scans for the opener at depth zero, parses the arguments against a token list truncated before it, then parses the block from the full one. Truncated only when a `do` is actually present, since the copy is not free.

- The REPL needed nothing, which is worth recording because it usually does: `expected end to close do` was already in its continuation list, so a multi-line `around "x" do` keeps reading. `AGENT.md` warns that any new multi-line construct must be added there — this one reuses `do`, so it was covered.

Before this morning the same shape was *divergent* rather than leaky, which is a nastier bug wearing a quieter face. Worth knowing before the ADR 0015 alias/append pair migrates, since that group mutates as part of what it tests.

Split across two `describe`s they would read as unrelated facts rather than the contrast the ADR exists to draw. - **What this does not fix, found by trying it: the spec runner still cannot hide its tally, and the reason is the language, not the trio.** A counter has to live somewhere, and only a block can reach an outer `mutable` — a method body gets a fresh scope, and there are no instance variables, no class variables, and no globals. - **A block's rebindings survive the block in the trio, through `yield`** — the accumulator pattern ADR 0001 licenses now computes the same answer on both oracles, where it was 2 direct against 0 hosted this morning. `def twice; yield; yield; end` with a `count += 1` block is the whole test, and it failed before it passed.

- The fix turned on finding the channel rather than writing one. A method body cannot reach its caller — `invoke_with_block` returns the caller's bindings exactly as captured — so an updated caller scope had nowhere to go. But `__block__` is already a caller-owned thing parked in the method's scope, so `yield` now rebinds it with the caller scope *as the block left it*, and `invoke_with_block` unpacks that on the way out. - `flow_back` decides what survives, and the prepend-only bindings list makes it nearly free: everything a block added sits in front of the scope it was handed, and the added entries worth keeping are exactly those whose name the caller already had. A new name is the block's own and dies; a name already out there is a rebinding of it. So block parameters and fresh block locals drop out by construction rather than by a list of exceptions.

- **The spec DSL says `specify`, not `it`** — mspec's own alias (`lib/mspec/runner/object.rb`), read rather than recalled, so it is borrowed vocabulary and not an invention. - **How the collision announced itself**, since the diagnostic did its job and is worth quoting: naming the DSL `it` stopped `report`'s `reject { it.ok }` from compiling — "block parameter it shadows method it — rename one" — the moment the spec DSL appeared, which is how the coverage hole got noticed at all. Ruby 4.0.6 was run rather than recalled: it resolves the same pair silently in favour of the block parameter, which is exactly the silence ADR 0017 rejected. The first fix was to name the parameter; that was treating the symptom, and the DSL got renamed instead.

- The spec file now carries **two harnesses on purpose**, with the reason written down: `report` counts, so every example must be a value it can hold, while `describe`/`specify` can only run and print. - **A divergence found by accident, undocumented, not fixed here: the two oracles refuse at different times.** The seed panics from `parser.rs` during parsing, so nothing runs; the trio's `parse_command` returns an `error_node` and defers, and since `run.pdx` parses the whole file before evaluating, the refusal fires mid-evaluation — after every earlier statement has already printed. - `yield` threaded through the trio and differentially pinned, closing the gap the same day it opened. `lexer.pdx` gained the keyword, `parser.pdx` gained `YieldNode` and a block on `CallNode`, and `evaluator.pdx` carries the block in the bindings as `__block__` — the same trick already used for `__self__` and `__home__` — paired with the scope it was written in, so a block still closes over its definition site rather than the method's fresh scope.

- One bug worth recording because the symptom pointed away from the cause: the **second** `yield` in a method reported "yield without a block". `evaluate_yield` was returning the block's bindings as the method's, which dropped `__block__` itself, so the next `yield` genuinely could not find it. The first `yield` always worked, which made it read like a block-passing failure rather than a scope-threading one.

- The differential test has its block **print** rather than accumulate into an outer `mutable`, deliberately: the trio discards a block's bindings, so an accumulator would diverge for a reason that has nothing to do with `yield`. Same choice, and same reason, as `value_methods.pdx`. That gap is now the most conspicuous thing standing between the seed and the trio.

- **`yield` works in the seed**: a user-defined method can be handed a `do ... end` block and run it. One test drove it — `def twice; yield; yield; end` incrementing a counter twice — and that one test was enough to surface the design question, which is the point of starting there.

- The question it surfaced: **a block closes over where it was written, not where it runs.** A method body gets a fresh scope with no outer locals (Ruby's rule, kept), so yielding into the method's own scope would hide the caller's `count` entirely. `yield` therefore carries the scope the block was written in, swaps it in for the duration, and swaps back — and whatever the block rebound flows back to the caller after the call returns, because blocks rebind outer mutables and that is the accumulator pattern ADR 0001 licenses. Getting that wrong is silent: the first attempt ran the block fine and simply lost every assignment it made.

- Only the bare form so far — `twice do ... end` — plus blocks on parenthesised calls. Paren-less calls *with arguments* still refuse a block with the existing never-guess error, `yield` takes no arguments yet, and the trio does not know about any of it. Deliberately left; each is its own small step.

- **A Portland language spec, written in Portland, running on both oracles** — `spec/language_spec.pdx`, 15 examples, `script/spec`, and a test that gates it. This is the **third oracle**: the differential harness proves the seed and the trio agree with *each other*, and cannot prove either agrees with what was *decided*, since a shared misreading of an ADR passes it. Every example cites the ADR it pins — floored division (0018), partial operations returning maybes (0010), slices never being maybes (0019), branchless `if` (0012), alias-proof append (0015), symbols not being strings (0023), enum payload destructuring (0022), and the stored-nil/missing-nil pair that is the whole point of the wrapper (0005). Verified to fail loudly by flipping one expectation.

- It is deliberately built from **only what the language has today** — no classes, no exceptions, no instance variables, no blocks on user-defined methods, none of which exist. An example is a *value* rather than a callback, which is what lets it run at all: `spec(...)` returns an `Example` struct instead of raising, because `panic` is the only crash Portland has and a spec run wants every failure rather than the first. The shape it is reaching for is rubyspec's `describe`/`it`, and the single thing between here and there is **`yield`**.

- Two limitations found by being the language's first real user, which is what a spec suite is for. **The seed cannot parse a multi-line argument list at all** — a newline anywhere inside the parens is a parse error, and `T.new(a: 1,` + newline reports "positional arguments cannot follow keyword arguments". Worth noting because the project's own style guide says multi-line calls should put each argument on its own line. And **the trio has no `require_relative`**, which is why the harness and the examples live in one file rather than two; splitting them is what that would buy.

- The harness avoids accumulating into a `mutable` from inside a block, deliberately and with a comment saying so: the trio discards a block's bindings, so a counter incremented that way is right under the seed and wrong under the trio — and a language spec whose own harness disagreed between oracles would be worse than none.

- `even?` and `odd?` say why they use Rust's raw `%` instead of `floored_modulo`, and a test now pins it. Portland's `%` is floored (ADR 0018) while Rust's truncates, so `-7 % 2` is 1 there and -1 here — but the two differ only in *sign*, never in zero-ness, and these arms only ever compare against zero. Correct by reasoning rather than luck, and the reasoning was invisible sitting six lines below a `floored_modulo` that exists for exactly this ADR. Someone could reasonably have "fixed" it, or copied the pattern somewhere the sign is observable. Negatives are now asserted against Ruby 4.0.6's answers, so the comment is enforced rather than hoped.

- **The trio speaks symbols, hash shorthand, and enums** — ADRs 0022 and 0023 are now seed *and* trio, differentially pinned, which closes the arc. `lexer.pdx` gained the same colon-binds-left-or-right rule as the seed; `parser.pdx` gained `SymbolNode`, `EnumCaseNode`, `EnumDefNode`, `EnumCasePatternNode`, hash shorthand, and `parse_enum`; `evaluator.pdx` gained construction and payload destructuring. - **The trio cannot manufacture a symbol, and that is the design working.** Guest symbols and enum cases are tagged arrays — `["__symbol__", name]` — because building a host symbol from a name at runtime would need `String#to_sym`, which ADR 0023 §3 removed precisely so that no symbol can be conjured from text. That is what makes membership checking possible rather than best-effort, and the trio holds a symbol's name as text like any other program does. The decision is being felt by its own compiler, which is the right outcome; the alternative, a privileged builtin only the trio may call, would be `to_sym` wearing a hat. Recorded in `architecture.md` under **Guest values the trio cannot spell**, alongside the pre-existing struct case.

- The gap is narrow and was measured rather than assumed: tagged values compare and match correctly, they only *print* differently, so anything computed from a symbol agrees byte-for-byte and only displaying one raw diverges. Closing it means teaching the trio to render values the way the seed's `inspect` does, which would fix guest structs at the same time.

- **`{name: "pdx"}` parses and runs** (ADR 0023 §1, third rung) — the most common modern Ruby hash form, which until now was a parse error every arriving Rubyist would hit in their first ten minutes. Keys are symbols, `config[:name]` reads them back, and mixed hashes work: `{"string" => 1, sym: 2}`. `{:name => "pdx"}` is refused with the rewrite named, since the shorthand is the only symbol-key spelling; the rocket survives for keys the shorthand cannot express.

- `inspect` writes a hash back the way it would be typed, which meant teaching it the shorthand — it was printing `{:name => "pdx"}`, the one spelling the language now rejects. **This is a deliberate parting from Ruby, verified by running 4.0.6 rather than assumed:** Ruby prints `{name: "pdx"}` for identifier-shaped symbol keys, and `{"odd key": 1}` for the rest. Portland prints `{:"odd key" => 1}` instead, because Ruby's quoted-label form does not exist here — ADR 0023 kept the rocket for exactly those keys.

- Symbols run in the seed (ADR 0023, second rung): `Value::Symbol`, evaluation, `case/in` patterns, and equality. `p :paid` prints `:paid` while `puts :paid` prints `paid`, and a symbol writes itself back as source — `:"odd key"` keeps its quotes because the name is not identifier-shaped. Comparison is equality only, and `:paid == "paid"` is `false` in the seed where the real compiler will refuse it outright, which is the usual dynamic-checking crudeness rather than a different decision. **`String#to_sym` does not exist**, pinned by a test: it is the omission that makes membership checking possible at all, since every symbol in a program must be written literally for the compiler to see it. Symbols also had to join the paren-less argument starters, so `p :paid` parses as a command call.

- Seed lexer: `:name` and `:"quoted"` lex as a `Symbol` token (ADR 0023, first rung). The rule that keeps this from needing a never-guess error is that **the colon binds left when it directly follows a name, and right otherwise** — so `{name: :paid}` lexes as label, then symbol, and `name:"pdx"` gets Ruby's own reading of a label plus a string rather than a name plus a quoted symbol. Operator symbols (`:+`, `:[]`) are not lexed at all, since `send`, `define_method`, and `&:` were their whole job. `?` and `!` are part of a name, so `:paid?` is one token. Nothing above the lexer knows about symbols yet: `:paid` remains a parse error, with a different message than before.

- `<<` and `[]=` update in place when the value is unshared, which takes parsing `parser.pdx` with the Portland parser from **29.7s to 5.4s** and the integration suite from 32.7s to 6.7s ([#34](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/34)). The fix is three lines of `Rc::make_mut`, but it only works because of a fourth: the old code **cloned** the current value out of its binding, which left the `Rc` refcount at 2, so `make_mut` would have copied the whole collection every time and changed nothing. Taking the value instead — `std::mem::replace(&mut binding.value, Value::Nil)` — makes the interpreter the sole owner, and then `make_mut` mutates in place. This is ADR 0015 and #12's RC-exactness thesis cashed out: immutability is preserved *because* a refcount of 1 proves nobody else can see the value, and where a value genuinely is shared (`other = list`) the refcount is 2 and `make_mut` copies, which is exactly correct. Verified rather than assumed: `b = a; a << 2` still leaves `b` at one element, the string and hash forms behave the same, and the whole differential suite stays byte-identical.

- ADR 0021: **namespaces decided** — Portland had none at all (`::` did not parse, `module` was not a keyword). `module` is a namespace **only**, with mixins deferred to a different keyword entirely, so `include Comparable` (real mixin) and `include Math` (typing convenience) can never be confused the way they are in Ruby. `::` names and `.` invokes, as a rule rather than a convention — `Statistics::mean(x)` is a never-guess error. Names are **always fully qualified**: no import, no aliasing, no injection, with lexical nesting the only shortening (an import can be added later; it cannot be removed). Both declaration forms exist — `module A::B` and nested blocks — and unlike Ruby they are **semantically identical**, dropping the `Module.nesting` footgun where outer constants vanish. Types nest in types; modules inside structs are an error. Evidence: `constant_path_node` appears 44,284 times across 78% of gems and `module_node` in 87.4%, while `super_node` is a 19.2% minority — namespacing is far more load-bearing in real Ruby than inheritance. - ADR 0020: heredocs decided — **squiggly `<<~` only**; `<<EOS` and `<<-EOS` are out. - ADR 0019: ranges decided — three answers. (1) Range patterns **count toward exhaustiveness**: sorted integer ranges with a beginless first, an endless last, and no gaps prove a `case` total with no `else`; gaps error, overlap stays legal (the cascading `..10 / ..100 / ..1000` idiom depends on first-match-wins), fully-shadowed arms were already errors, arm order is a lint someday. (3) **A range spans a newline only where one reading exists** — slices and patterns stay paren-free (`array[1..]`, `in ..10`), while both ambiguous shapes error and name their readings: a trailing `1..` at end of line, and a line-initial `..4` after a complete expression.
