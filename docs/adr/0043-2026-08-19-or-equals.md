# 0043 — `||=`: the or-guard's question as a rebind

- **Status:** Accepted (built in both oracles, 2026-08-19) — closes the last of [#73](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/73)'s five spellings
- **Date:** 2026-08-19
- **Issue:** [#73](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/73)

## Context

The compound family had `+= -= *= /= %=` and `<<`; `||=` was the missing member, skipped twice during earlier sessions and ruled here. Its sibling `&&=` is deliberately **out**: its maybe personality would silently decide `and`-on-a-maybe semantics — never stated in any ADR — as a side effect, and its common idiom was obsoleted by `&.` a decade ago; [ruby_research#2](https://github.com/portlandlang/ruby_research/issues/2) sizes whether it is a living idiom or a fossil before any ruling.

## Decision

**`x ||= fallback` is sugar for `x = x || fallback`, and nothing else.** Both parsers desugar at parse (the seed to `Logical`/`Or` over a rebind, the compiler identically); the evaluators never see a new form, so drift is structurally impossible. Every rule is inherited:

- **On a maybe, it is the or-guard's question as a rebind** — absent takes the fallback, present is kept, and the binding is plain below (ADR 0040 §3's narrowing shape). This is the meaning Rubyists reach for, and here it is the _only_ absence meaning, because absence is only ever `nil`-the-maybe.
- **`mutable` is required**, like every compound: `name ||= x` on an immutable refuses with the standing wording, pinned.
- **An unbound name refuses** — no Ruby-style spooky nil-initialization of undefined locals; the right-hand `x` evaluates first and refuses as undefined.
- **Hash slots ride the existing `[]=` rebinding sugar**; instance-variable memoization — Ruby's headline `||=` idiom — waits on the object-model arc regardless of this ADR.

**Stated honestly: on a plain Boolean, `||=` is boolean-or, and Ruby's false-clobbering outcome reproduces** (`flag = false; flag ||= true` answers `true`). A first presentation of this ruling claimed ADR 0007's refusal would catch it; probing showed that refusal guards `Boolean?` — a _maybe_ of Boolean, where presence and truth are two different no's — not plain Booleans, where `||` is legitimate. The actual guard against the trap is the **static** Boolean?-under-`or` check, which sits in #9's deferred inventory and was explicitly trimmed until narrowing proves itself (ADR 0040 §3). Until it lands, the spec pins the boolean-or outcome as fact; when it lands, the maybe-of-Boolean cases become refusals and plain-Boolean cases stay boolean-or, which is the honest split Ruby never had.

## Consequences

- [#73](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/73) closes: `until`, the postfix loops, and `loop do` shipped (ADR 0037), subjectless `case` declined with reconsideration ([#84](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/84)), `||=` shipped here.
- A non-difference from Ruby in the common cases; the divergences are the loud inherited ones (immutable and unbound refusals) plus the future static split on maybe-Booleans — [mutability.md](../ruby/mutability.md) carries the compound family.
- `&&=` refuses as unknown syntax with no designed wording yet; its fate follows the corpus report.
