# 0071 — A symbol may be made from text; a declared vocabulary still refuses it

- **Status:** Accepted (ruled by the deciding user 2026-11-03 in discussion; supersedes [ADR 0023](0023-2026-07-25-symbols.md) §3 and the interpolated-symbol sentence of §2; built on both implementations 2026-11-03 under [#184](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/184) — `to_sym`, `intern`, and interpolated symbols; the checked-position refusal of ruling 2 waits on a position that checks a vocabulary, which no struct field or argument does yet, and is [#200](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/200))
- **Date:** 2026-11-03
- **Supersedes in part:** [ADR 0023](0023-2026-07-25-symbols.md) §2 (interpolated symbols) and §3 (`to_sym`)

## Context

[ADR 0023](0023-2026-07-25-symbols.md) §3 cut `String#to_sym` and called it "the load-bearing omission": every symbol in a Portland program is written literally, which is what lets the checker refuse `:pendign` where a `Purchase::Status` is expected. The ledger calls the cut "the loudest single break" in the symbols file, with no rewrite. Re-examined on 2026-11-03 while deciding JSON's `symbolize_names:` ([#182](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/182)), the four reasons the record gives for the cut hold up unequally:

1. **Checkability.** Real, but narrower than stated. ADR 0023 itself concedes that *open positions stay unchecked*: `config[:nmae]` is a valid symbol that misses with a maybe. Hash keys are where nearly every runtime-made symbol goes, so the cut protects exactly the positions a runtime symbol would never reach — enum arguments, `case/in` arms over a vocabulary — and protects nothing at the positions it would. The protection can live in the checker instead, as a type.
1. **The metaprogramming door.** Spent. `send`, `const_get`, `define_method`, and `instance_variable_get` — the consumers that made a runtime name dangerous — are gone regardless ([metaprogramming.md](../ruby/metaprogramming.md)). A symbol flowing into `hash[key]` is not metaprogramming.
1. **Erlang's atom table.** Moot here. Ruby has collected dynamic symbols since 2.2; the seed represents a symbol as `Value::Symbol(String)`, a tagged string with no global table, and under RC-exact memory a runtime symbol dies with its last reference.
1. **Use versus mention.** The genuine one — a symbol says "this is a name the program controls, not data" — and a taste argument. Principle 2 says taste defaults to Ruby and a removal must name what it buys; this one bought the checking in reason 1, which the checker can provide another way.

The deciding user's own position: `"pending".to_sym` and `JSON.parse(text, symbolize_names: true)` are more joyous in many situations, and Portland exists to keep Ruby's joy where it costs nothing a principle protects.

**The corpus.** [ruby_research](https://github.com/portlandlang/ruby_research)'s `symbols-from-text` report, run 2026-11-03 over a seeded sample of 5,000 of RubyGems.org's 196,982 gems (the source cache had been pruned, so a full run would re-fetch 95 GB; the sample is reproducible): **27.5% of gems build a symbol from text somewhere.** `to_sym` is in 23.1% of gems and rising — 18.4% of 2015–2019 gems, 28.0% of 2020+ — with interpolated symbols in 6.0% (9.6% of 2020+), `symbolize_names:` in 1.9% (4.0% of 2020+), and `transform_keys(&:to_sym)` in 3.7% of 2020+ gems from nothing before. Of the 10,852 `to_sym`/`intern` sites, **95.1% are called on a variable or a call** — text the program did not write, the case the cut was aimed at — and 0.5% on a string literal that could have been `:name`. Where the runtime symbol goes, over 13,241 sites: a hash key 25.2%, a membership test 13.0%, a `case` subject 11.3%, a binding 12.1%, another method's argument 6.9%, a comparison with a literal symbol 0.9%, a metaprogramming call 15.9% — and that last bucket is mostly interpolated symbols (1,689 of their 2,389 sites, the `instance_variable_get(:"@#{name}")` idiom), which die with metaprogramming regardless. So roughly half of all runtime symbols feed open positions the proposal allows outright, a sixth feeds code Portland has already removed, and the `case` subjects and arguments — a fifth — are exactly where the carve-out's gate goes. The gems with the most sites are generated API clients (ory-client, onfido, shortcut_client_ruby) mapping attribute names, a shape a linter rewrites mechanically.

## Decision

1. **`String#to_sym` exists**, and `intern` is its twin ([ADR 0036](0036-2026-08-18-the-famous-twins-ship.md)). `"pending".to_sym` is `:pending`. Interpolated symbols come back with it — `:"prefix_#{name}"` is sugar for `"prefix_#{name}".to_sym`, and ADR 0023 §2's reason for cutting them was §3's.
1. **A symbol made from text is a `Symbol` of no declared vocabulary.** It may stand anywhere a symbol is a plain value: a hash key or index, a comparison with a literal symbol, a `case` subject, an argument, a binding. What it may not do is pass where a declared vocabulary is expected: `purchase.with(status: name.to_sym)` refuses at build time, in the error voice of [ADR 0047](0047-2026-09-22-the-error-voice.md): `'name.to_sym' is a Symbol of no declared vocabulary — match it against Purchase::Status with case/in first`.
1. **`case/in` is the gate.** An exhaustive `case/in` over a vocabulary's cases narrows the subject to each literal inside its arm, as it already narrows maybes ([ADR 0040](0040-2026-08-19-inference-the-design-core.md)), so `case name.to_sym in :paid then purchase.with(status: :paid) in :pending then … end` is how data becomes a checked name — and the boundary between data and name, which the cut placed at the constructor, now sits at the one place it matters, written out where a reader can see it. A `case/in` over a vocabulary with a runtime symbol as subject still needs an `else`, since the text may name no case.
1. **Membership checking is unchanged for literals.** `:pendign` where a `Purchase::Status` is expected refuses as before; `:paid == "paid"` stays a compile error; operator symbols stay out, since their consumers are gone.
1. **`symbolize_names:` follows** ([#182](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/182)): a parsed document's keys may be symbols, and `document[:name]` is an open position.

## Consequences

- ADR 0023 §3 is superseded, and §2's "Interpolated symbols are out" with it; its §1 (hash shorthand), the rest of §2, and §4 stand. The checked-vocabulary model of ADR 0022 is untouched; what moves is where the check is enforced.
- Inference gains a distinction it did not have: a `Symbol` type whose name is statically known (a literal, or a `case/in` arm's narrowing) and one whose name is not. The existing membership check refuses the second at a checked position; nothing else changes.
- Ledger: [symbols.md](../ruby/symbols.md) loses its loudest break — `.to_sym` ports verbatim, and the one new refusal, at a checked position, names the `case/in` rewrite. [architecture.md](../architecture.md)'s note that the hosted compiler "cannot build a symbol from text and that is the right outcome" is retired; the compiler may now call `to_sym` like any program.
- Not built here. [#184](https://github.com/portlandlang/portland/issues/184) builds `to_sym`, `intern`, and interpolated symbols on both implementations with the checked-position refusal, and ports ruby/spec's `core/string/to_sym_spec.rb`, `intern_spec.rb`, and `core/symbol/` rows that waited on it.
- Rejected: keeping the cut and offering an escape hatch (`Symbol.from(text)` or similar) — a second spelling for the same thing, and the hatch would be used everywhere `to_sym` is, which is the use-versus-mention argument lost twice.
